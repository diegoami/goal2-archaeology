## 2. The unit, the scaling and the records' bound (read, measured)

- **Unit.** A prompt's wait is a number of loop passes, never seconds. `wait_or_key(n)` is `n` polls, each followed by `1E09(10)`, and `1E09(10)` is 10 × `DS:A9FF` passes of its own loop. `DS:A9FF` is the passes of the *calibration* loop in one BIOS tick (54.9 ms) divided by 55, so one "ms" of `1E09` is 1/55 of a tick if the delay loop's pass takes as long as the calibration loop's. The nominal waits are 600 × 10 = 6000 ms (replay, save-goal, summary) and 1000 × 10 = 10 000 ms per pass of the taker prompt. static:static/goal-exe/code/seg_214B_04.asm:57 static:static/goal-exe/code/seg_214B_04.asm:66 E015-r0013
- **Scaling with `DS:A9FF`.** The count is read from memory on every pass, so a wait is proportional to `DS:A9FF`; `DS:A9FF` is written only by `1DA7`, once, at start-up (F006: 0x2c5 = 709 at fixed 5000, 0x10b3 = 4275 at fixed 30000 over fresh launches, ratio 6.03 for 6.0 in cycles; 0x2d9 to 0x32c at `max`; every savestate restore keeps the state's value). static:static/goal-exe/code/seg_214B_04.asm:100 static:static/goal-exe/code/seg_214B_04.asm:102 static:findings/F006-cycles-and-host-speed-do-not-change-a-match.md:35
- **Scaling with the emulated CPU speed.** The wait in host time is the nominal wait times (cycles per host second at the calibration) / (cycles per host second now): equal when the game was calibrated at the speed it runs at (a fresh launch at `cycles=fixed 8000`, which is how E015-r0013 was started: r0013 note 1, 05:05:13 UTC; no start state was restored), longer when the speed was lowered after start-up (r0017 note 7, 05:01:05 UTC: about 20 presses of Ctrl+F11), shorter when it was raised or when the game was calibrated at the slow start of `max` and then ran fast. Under `max` a 6 s prompt can fall well below a second. This follows from F006's table and the listing; no sitting measured a prompt against a known `DS:A9FF` (the extractor does not record it).
- **A second, smaller factor (likely, not measured).** The calibration loop is 7 instructions per pass (`add`, `adc`, `mov`, `xor`, `mov es`, `cmp`, `je`) and the delay loop 8 (`add`, `adc`, `les`, `mov`, `cmp`, `jne`, `cmp`, `jne`), so if the emulator charges one cycle per instruction a nominal ms is 8/7 of an emulated ms (6.9 s and 11.4 s).
